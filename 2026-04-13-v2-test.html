The Ancient City That Rewrote Everything We Knew About Civilisation — Discovered in 1922, Still Yielding Secrets
In 1922, R.D. Banerji stood in the ruins of Mohenjo-daro and realised he was looking at a city that predated everything on record — a fully planned urban settlement with standardised bricks, indoor plumbing, and grid-layout streets, built 4,500 years ago in what is now Pakistan. A civilisation of 5 million people, larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia combined at their peak, that we knew nothing about until 100 years ago. Archaeologists are still excavating it today. The culture that built it had no monarchy, no military, and no monumental temples — just... cities. Extraordinary, functional, egalitarian cities.
The dinner-table version: The largest civilisation of the ancient world — bigger than Egypt, bigger than Mesopotamia — existed for 700 years and had indoor plumbing before Rome did. We only found out it existed in 1922. They had no army, no kings, no temples. Just really good city planning. Nobody knows why they vanished.
