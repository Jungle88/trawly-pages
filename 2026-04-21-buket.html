🔥 The Big One
Estrogen patches are running out — because demand for HRT just doubled
Prescriptions for estrogen-based hormone replacement therapy more than doubled between 2018 and 2026, according to data from Truveta. The surge followed the FDA's removal of black box warnings from HRT packaging — warnings that were based on the flawed Women's Health Initiative study and had suppressed prescribing for two decades. Now manufacturers of estrogen patches are struggling to keep up, with the FDA working to address the shortage.

🌿 Women's Health
RESEARCH
Ageing rewires immune gene expression in women — and could explain the autoimmune burden
A new study in Nature profiling gene expression across age groups has found that women's immune systems undergo pronounced shifts with age that increase susceptibility to autoimmune disorders — changes that are distinct from those seen in men. The research also identified age-related increases in cancer vulnerability in men. The two trajectories diverge sharply, suggesting sex-specific immune ageing programmes that may have evolved separately.
Why it matters: This is mechanistic evidence for why autoimmune conditions (lupus, RA, Hashimoto's, Sjögren's) cluster so heavily in women, and why perimenopause is often the inflection point. The drop in oestrogen during perimenopause removes a key immunomodulatory signal — this study begins to explain what the immune system does in response.
🎯 Why this matters to you As a hospital scientist who works with cancer genetics, you'll recognise the methodology — transcriptomic profiling with age-stratified cohorts. What's notable is the perimenopause-specific immune shift this kind of data implies. If you're in early perimenopause, this is the immune window that changes.

🧬 Cancer Genetics & AI
FIELD
How AI is rewriting the logic of cancer genomics — from biomarkers to master regulators
Time's April 15 deep-dive on AI in oncology covers a development that goes beyond pattern recognition: systems trained on genomic databases spanning tens of thousands of sequenced cancer samples can now identify master regulatory patterns that are active specifically in cancer cells — patterns invisible to classical biomarker approaches. Unlike single-gene biomarkers, these regulatory signatures cut across tumour types, potentially enabling pan-cancer therapeutics.
Why it matters: The shift from single-biomarker logic to regulatory network logic is a genuine paradigm shift in oncology. This is the direction your field is heading — cytogenetic data, which captures chromosomal-level regulatory disruption, becomes vastly more interpretable when placed within AI-derived network maps of which pathways are dysregulated across cancer types.
🎯 Why this matters to you As a hospital cytogeneticist, you currently interpret chromosomal abnormalities in the context of established clinical correlates. The next iteration of that work will be AI-assisted — not to replace your interpretive judgement, but to contextualise karyotypic findings within system-wide regulatory profiles derived from tens of thousands of cases. The PANORAMA study (Lancet Oncology, 2026) already demonstrated AI matching radiologist performance on pancreatic cancer CT detection; the comparable cytogenomics papers are 12-18 months behind.
🔖 Time Magazine · time.com
JOURNAL
Precision oncology in the age of AI — what drug discovery is teaching clinical translation
A review in BJC Reports (Nature portfolio) examines how AI-driven drug discovery pipelines — trained on genetic and clinical datasets — are accelerating the identification of resistance mechanisms and treatment targets. Notably, the paper discusses predictive models for castration resistance in advanced prostate cancer using machine learning on genetic and clinical data, demonstrating that multi-variable AI models outperform single-marker approaches for predicting treatment trajectories.
Why it matters: This is the clinical translation paper that bridges the AI-discovery space and actual treatment decisions. The federated learning approach — training models across multiple institutions without sharing patient data — is likely how cytogenomics AI will enter clinical practice in Australia. The Cancer AI Alliance's eight pilot projects mentioned in the ASCO Post this month are the first wave of exactly this.

New Montessori research: family engagement is the underrated variable in early childhood outcomes
A study published April 8 in the International Journal of Early Years Education examined what distinguishes high-performing Montessori early childhood environments from average ones. The answer wasn't teacher training or materials — it was the quality and nature of family engagement. 🌱 Apothecary & Herbal Medicine
RCT
Ashwagandha-based multi-herb formula reduces stress, anxiety, and improves sleep — randomised controlled trial
A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial published April 17 in Trials (doi: 10.1186/s13063-026-09495-9) tested a multi-herb formula combining ashwagandha root with complementary adaptogenic plants for stress modulation. The trial demonstrated statistically significant reductions in perceived stress, anxiety scores, and objective sleep quality measures compared to placebo. Effect sizes were modest but consistent — and notably, the multi-herb combination outperformed ashwagandha alone, suggesting synergistic mechanisms between the plant constituents.
Why it matters: This is the kind of evidence you'd want before putting something in an apothecary garden rather than a supplement jar. Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is well-suited to Sydney's climate and can be grown year-round in a sheltered position. The HPA-axis modulation mechanism (cortisol reduction) is relevant to perimenopause-related sleep disruption, where elevated evening cortisol is often a driver.
🎯 Why this matters to you You're actively planning your apothecary garden in Sydney right now — autumn is actually a good planting window for ashwagandha seedlings if you can source them (search Sydney for "Withania somnifera" or "Indian ginseng" at herbal nurseries). This RCT gives you the clinical basis for prioritising it over less-evidenced options like maca or rhodiola, which have thinner trial data.

EVIDENCE REVIEW
Do adaptogens really work? The science, separated from the supplement industry
USA Today's science desk reviewed the adaptogen evidence base — distinguishing between the categories that have genuine RCT support (ashwagandha, rhodiola, panax ginseng) and those riding marketing momentum without trial data. The key finding: adaptogens work via HPA-axis modulation, reducing cortisol output during stress exposure. They do not dramatically change baseline cortisol in non-stressed individuals — a distinction that separates them from stimulants and explains why they work better in high-allostatic-load conditions (i.e. perimenopause).
Why it matters: This is a good calibration piece for garden planning — it helps you distinguish which herbs to prioritise based on actual evidence. Ashwagandha and rhodiola have the strongest RCT backing; tulsi (holy basil), while widely sold, has much thinner evidence. Worth reading before buying seeds.
🔖 USA Today · usatoday.com
📈 Still Climbing
📈 Still Climbing — posted 13 days ago, still driving discussion as women race to fill HRT prescriptions
HRT
Hormone therapy prescriptions surged after FDA removed its decades-old black box warning
Bloomberg reported April 8 that the FDA's removal of the black box warning from menopausal hormone therapy packaging — a warning that was attached following the 2002 Women's Health Initiative study and its subsequent reanalysis — has triggered a rapid surge in prescribing. The original WHI findings were later found to have been misapplied to younger, peri-menopausal women: the study cohort was significantly older, and the risk-benefit calculation in women entering menopause in their late 40s is materially different. Prescribers who had avoided HRT for two decades are now issuing scripts they'd deferred.
Why it matters: The WHI story is one of the most consequential errors in modern evidence-based medicine — it shaped a generation of GP prescribing behaviour based on a cohort mismatch. The correction is now playing out in real time, but the supply chain (estrogen patches, Big One above) hasn't caught up. The clinical window has opened; the pharmacy shelf hasn't.
🔖 Bloomberg · bloomberg.com

⚡ Editor's Corner
What happens when medicine spends 20 years getting something wrong
The estrogen patch shortage and HRT prescription surge are both downstream consequences of a single methodological error made in 2002 — the WHI study applied to the wrong population. Two decades of undertreated perimenopause, suppressed prescribing, and women told their symptoms were "normal." The correction is now arriving, but the supply chain wasn't ready. There's something instructive in that for medicine generally: the cost of overcorrection to fear is paid for a long time after the fear is disproved.
