🃏 Wildcard
Outside your usual read. Intellectually worth 2 minutes.
New Zealand Parrots Independently Invented Toll Booths
Road workers near a tunnel by Milford Sound kept finding their traffic cones in weird places — rearranged, sometimes actively rerouting traffic. They checked the cameras. Kea parrots. These big alpine parrots would listen for cars coming through the tunnel before moving the cones, timing it so cars would have to stop. Why? Stopped cars mean humans getting out. Humans getting out means food. The transport agency's response was equally unhinged: they switched to heavier cones the birds couldn't move, and then built dedicated "kea gyms" — enrichment structures to keep them occupied. A flock of kea is officially called a "circus" or a "curiosity." Whoever named them met one.
The dinner-table version: New Zealand parrots learned that moving road cones stops cars, stopping cars gets food — and the government had to build them a playground to stop them doing it.
