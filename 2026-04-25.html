Outside your usual read. Intellectually worth 2 minutes.
The Hubble Space Telescope turned 36 yesterday. It's still pointing at things 13 billion light-years away.
On April 24, 1990 — exactly 36 years ago yesterday — Space Shuttle Discovery carried Hubble into orbit. Scientists discovered a flaw in the primary mirror weeks later. A 1993 repair mission sent astronauts into open space to fix a contact-lens-sized error with their hands, while floating. Since then: 1.6 million observations, 21,000+ peer-reviewed papers, images of galaxies that formed 400 million years after the Big Bang. Annual cost: ~$100M. Still running.
The dinner-table version: Humanity built a time machine that shows us what the universe looked like 13 billion years ago — and when it broke, we flew up and fixed it with our hands.
🧒 Teach Leonardo: Ask him: "If you built a telescope and sent it to space but it had a tiny mistake, how would you fix it?" Then tell him astronauts put on spacesuits and flew up to fix it with their own hands — while floating in space. Hubble works because people didn't give up on something they believed in.
