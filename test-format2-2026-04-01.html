Karpathy: LLM Personalization Is Broken — Memory Makes Models Worse

In what might be the most practically useful observation of the week, Karpathy identified a fundamental flaw in how every LLM handles personalization: "A single question from 2 months ago about some topic can keep coming up as some kind of a deep interest of mine with undue mentions in perpetuity."

He's describing a problem every power user has felt: you ask about cryptocurrency once, and suddenly every response mentions your "interest in crypto." The LLMs are trying too hard. They treat every piece of memory as equally important, creating a distorted model of who you are that gets worse over time, not better.

With 21,188 likes and 3,338 bookmarks, this struck a massive chord. Karpathy notes it's not specific to any one LLM — it's something deeper, possibly baked into how models are trained. "A lot of the information in the context window is relevant to the task, so the LLMs develop a bias to use what is given."
