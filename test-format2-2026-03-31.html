🃏 Wildcard
Outside your usual read. Intellectually worth 2 minutes.
Bird brains: they have no neocortex — but they're smarter than most mammals
For decades, "bird brain" was an insult. Then neuroscientists looked closer. Crows can solve multi-step puzzles, New Caledonian crows craft tools from materials they've never seen before, and Clark's nutcrackers can remember the exact location of 9,000 seed caches across an entire winter. The remarkable part: birds don't have the neocortex we assumed was required for higher cognition. They built a completely different neural architecture that arrives at the same destination. The brain's purpose — intelligence — can be implemented in multiple ways. The structure is just one path, not the only path. This paper resurfaced on HN this week with 236 upvotes.
The dinner-table version: "A crow has never seen a university, doesn't have our brain structure, and will outsmart most people in a tool-use test. Intelligence isn't about the architecture — it's about the pressure that shaped it."
