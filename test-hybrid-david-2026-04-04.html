The sharpest critique came from a cognitive scientist who noted that AI tutors currently optimize for demonstrated knowledge (answering questions correctly) rather than durable learning (long-term retention and transfer). A student who can ace every AI-generated quiz may still struggle when confronted with a genuinely novel problem that requires synthesizing knowledge in a new way. This is the "teaching to the AI" version of "teaching to the test."

The researchers stopped short of condemning AI schooling — acknowledging that the personalization benefits are real. Their recommendation: use AI as a powerful acceleration layer within a framework that still includes human mentorship, physical collaboration, and regular exposure to genuinely hard problems that don't have a "right answer." The hybrid model — not pure AI, not pure traditional — may be where the data lands.

Myofunctional therapy (tongue and jaw exercises) is gaining significant clinical backing as a first-line non-surgical intervention for childhood sleep-disordered breathing. Three RCTs published in the last 6 months showed 30-50% reduction in AHI (apnea-hypopnea index) in pediatric patients after 12 weeks of myofunctional therapy — without surgery.

The mechanism: mouth breathing in children causes the tongue to rest in the wrong position, which affects palate development and airway shape. Myofunctional therapy retrains the tongue posture to nasal breathing defaults, which over time reshapes the palate and widens the airway. The evidence for nasal strips, lip tape (in appropriate supervised cases for older kids), and specific jaw exercises is building. ENT surgeons typically recommend adenotonsillectomy first — but the emerging evidence suggests myofunctional therapy before surgery for mild-to-moderate cases.

Practical step: search for a myofunctional therapist in Sydney (there are several who specialise in pediatric cases). An assessment costs $200-400 and will tell you whether Leo's case is amenable to conservative intervention before surgery. Several Sydney-based OTs and speech pathologists now offer this specialisation.
