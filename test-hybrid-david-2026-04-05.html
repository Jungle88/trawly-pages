Agentic Marketing: When the AI Doesn't Just Suggest — It Ships the Campaign

TreasureData published a comprehensive breakdown this week of what they're calling "Agentic Marketing" — and it's the clearest articulation of where the Enviz-style AI-for-sales thesis is heading. The distinction they draw is important: most "AI marketing" today is still a human with a smart assistant (copilot suggests a subject line; marketer approves). The AI Tutor Paradox: GPT-4 Boosts Kids' Scores by 127% — Then Removes 17% When Access Is Removed

The OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 published a finding that should be required reading for every parent considering AI-first homeschooling: in a study in Türkiye, GPT-4 boosted student short-term performance by 48% with a standard interface — and by 127% with a tutoring-style interface. Extraordinary numbers. But when access was removed, those students scored 17% worse than peers who had learned without AI at all.

The finding reframes the homeschooling-with-AI question from "does it work?" to "what are we optimising for?" If the goal is test score maximization, AI tutors like Khanmigo win decisively. If the goal is genuine comprehension and the ability to reason without assistance, the design of the AI interaction matters enormously. The key variable: is the AI doing the thinking for the child, or teaching the child how to think?

The distinction Khanmigo makes explicitly — Sal Khan has described it publicly — is that Khanmigo refuses to give answers. It asks questions back. It says "what do you think?" before offering a path forward. That Socratic approach is what produces durable learning rather than performance enhancement that disappears when the tool does.

The Australian context is different — NSW kindergarten curriculum still includes substantial play-based learning — but the global pressure toward early academic benchmarking is bleeding across every Western school system.
